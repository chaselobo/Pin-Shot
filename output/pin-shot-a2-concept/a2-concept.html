Concept A2 Flap-Back Gate

Pull the pin.
The shot drops.
Drink through it.

The shot fills the whole cap. A flat gate on the end of the pin holds it there. Pull the ring, the shot falls through a 22 mm throat into the mixer, the lid flips open, and you drink back up through the empty shot cup. A small flap closes the slot behind the gate so nothing leaks when you tip it.

The parts that move

  1. Gate pin. Ring, stem and a flat paddle in one molded piece. It comes completely free.
  2. Shot cup. Spans the whole cap and funnels down to a 22 mm throat. 44 mL sealed under the lid.
  3. Two gaskets. The paddle is clamped between an upper gasket (shot side) and a lower gasket (mixer side). That's the only seal between the two liquids on the shelf.
  4. Flap. Rides flat on top of the paddle while it's in. Once the paddle passes, it swings down onto its seat. When you tip the bottle, the drink pushes it harder onto the seat.
  5. Exit seal. A soft slit in the cap wall that closes behind the paddle. It's the second barrier after the flap.
  6. Latch tab. The lid's long tab runs down the side of the cap. The stem passes through it, so the lid can't open until the paddle is all the way out. The shot always drops before the lid opens.
  7. Snap hinge. The lid's hinge flips it open once the tab is free, the same way a sports-cap lid pops.

Order is set by geometry

Throat opens at about 4 mm of pull. Flap drops at about 33 mm. Lid releases at about 50 mm. A half pull can drop the shot, but it can't open the lid.

How it gets filled

The cap becomes a sealed shot capsule before it ever meets the bottle. The bottle line stays standard.

  1. MoldFive parts. Rubber gaskets and the exit seal are molded straight onto the collar and the shot cup insert.
  2. Load the pinSlide the gate pin in through the exit seal. The paddle pushes the flap up flat as it goes in.
  3. Close the gateSnap the shot cup insert into the collar. It clamps the paddle between the two gaskets.
  4. Fill the shotWith the cap upright and the lid open, fill 44 mL of spirit.
  5. Seal the capsuleClose the lid. The plug ring seals the cup and the tab snaps over the stem. Now it can be handled in any orientation.
  6. Fill the mixerStandard fill of the mixer into the bottle, still or carbonated.
  7. Cap itScrew the shot capsule onto the bottle like a sports cap. Then sleeve, date code and pack.

Carbonated flavors need one more thing

All four flavors as named are usually fizzy. Soda pushes up on the paddle across the whole throat:

3.8 cm² × 3 bar ≈ 115 N on the paddle

That likely means a 25–35 N pull, which is stiff and changes with temperature. A still mixer has almost none of this load.

Options, cheapest first

  • Add the hiss groove to the same pin (concept A3). A small groove and a click stop vent the gas before the paddle moves. No new parts. Recommended
  • Lighter carbonation, around 1.5–2 bar. That roughly halves the load and still tastes sparkling. Recipe change
  • Launch with still versions and add fizz later. Narrower range
