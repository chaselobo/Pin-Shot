PIN SHOT

Gate Pin, drink-through

The shot fills the whole top of the cap. Pull the gate and the shot drops through a round throat into the mixer. Then you drink back up through that same throat and the empty shot cup. Below is the shared layout, a base design, and three add-ons that fix its weak spots. Press Play or drag the slider on any of them.

Gate pin (the part you pull) Liquor, 44 mL (tinted) Mixer Seals, flap Gas path

What the drink-through layout changes

  • The shot cup is the drinking cup. No separate drinking channel, so the cap gets simpler. It also gets taller, roughly 45 mm, to hold a full shot across its width.
  • A 22 mm throat. That's the same bore as an ordinary soda bottle neck, so it drinks normally and air and liquid can swap through it. The shot glugs straight down and no air-return channel is needed.
  • A bigger paddle, about 30 × 30 mm, clamped between an upper gasket (shot side) and a lower gasket (mixer side).
  • The lid seals the top of the shot cup. Its long latch tab runs down the side of the cap and is held by the pin. That tab is your grenade spoon.
  • The slot now opens into the drinking path. After the paddle is out, the drink can reach the slot every time you tip the bottle. Add-on A2 fixes that.

Why carbonation matters here

The mixer pushes up on the paddle across the whole throat. With a carbonated mixer:

3.8 cm² × 3 bar ≈ 115 N on the paddle

With normal plastic-on-rubber friction, that's roughly 25–35 N of pull on top of the gasket squeeze. That's stiff, and it varies with temperature. A still mixer has almost none of this load. Add-ons A3 and A4 are two ways to take the pressure off before the paddle moves.

A1
Base design

Funnel Gate

Full-width shot cup, 22 mm throat, flat paddle underneath.

One pullFewest partsSlot in drink path

How it works

  1. Ring, stem and paddle are one molded piece that comes completely free.
  2. The shot cup spans the whole cap and funnels down to a 22 mm throat.
  3. The paddle is clamped between an upper and a lower gasket. Shot above, mixer below.
  4. A slit seal in the cap wall wipes the paddle on the way out and closes behind it.
  5. The stem holds the lid's long latch tab. When the paddle clears it, the lid is free.
  6. The lid's spring hinge flips it open, leaving the shot cup as your drinking cup.
Pull stroke
About 50 mm, until the paddle clears the latch tab
Shot drain
Straight through the 22 mm throat. No vent channel.
Parts
Ring-paddle, 2 gaskets, slit seal, cap with shot cup, lid

Main risk

The open slot. Every time you tip the bottle to drink, the drink fills the paddle track and only the slit seal stops it.

First test

Printed cap on a wide-mouth bottle, dyed water, still mixer. Pull, then drink-tilt it 20 times and count drips at the slot.

A2
Add-on · fixes the slot

Flap-Back

A small flap swings shut behind the paddle and seals the track.

Slot sealedPressure helps it sealOne extra flap

How it works

  1. Same ring and paddle as A1.
  2. A thin flap hangs on a living hinge at the throat end of the paddle track. While the paddle is in, it lies folded flat in a recess above the paddle.
  3. Once the paddle passes, the flap swings down onto a rubber seat. Now the drink, when you tip the bottle, pushes the flap harder onto its seat. It works like a one-way valve.
  4. The slit seal at the wall stays as a second barrier.
Adds
One flap. It can be molded as part of the cap with a living hinge.
Pull feel
Same as A1
Leak path
Two barriers in series: flap, then slit seal

Main risk

After months folded flat, the hinge may take a set and not swing back far enough. Test it after a hot-storage soak. A soft rubber flap would recover better than a stiff plastic one.

First test

Print the flap in a flexible resin, store the assembled cap at 40 °C for a week, then pull it and tilt-test.

A3
Add-on · carbonated flavors

Hiss Notch

Click, hiss, pull. The first few millimeters let the gas out before the gate moves.

Light pullSound cueNo extra partsTwo-stage pull

How it works

  1. Same ring and paddle, now with a detent bump on the stem.
  2. A shallow groove is molded into the underside of the paddle.
  3. The first 4–5 mm of pull stops at a click. In that position the groove bridges the lower gasket, so bottle gas escapes along the paddle track.
  4. The gas hisses out past the exit seal. Pressure drops to zero while the shot is still sealed above the paddle.
  5. Pull through the click and the paddle slides out under almost no load. Shot drops, lid flips. A2's flap can still be included.
Adds
A groove and a bump on the same molded pin. No new parts.
Pull feel
Click, hiss, then an easy pull
Carbonation
Vented before the paddle moves, so the shot lands in flat-pressure soda and foams less

Main risk

If the bottle was just shaken, mixer foam could spit out with the gas. The hiss path should be narrow and rise uphill before the exit.

First test

Carbonate water to about 3 bar in the test bottle. Measure pull force with and without the groove, then repeat after shaking.

A4
Add-on · carbonated flavors, option 2

Balanced Vent

A gas-only membrane keeps the shot cup at the same pressure as the bottle.

One smooth pullLight pullBought-in membrane

How it works

  1. Same ring and paddle as A1, with a single smooth pull.
  2. A small vent disk made of PTFE membrane sits in a channel in the cap wall. Gas passes through it but liquid can't. The same kind of vent is used on chemical and pharmaceutical caps.
  3. The channel links the bottle's headspace to the shot cup's headspace. Both sit at the same pressure, so the paddle carries almost no load from the soda.
  4. When the lid opens, both chambers vent together through the wide top.
Adds
A vent disk welded into a molded channel
Pull feel
One easy pull, same at any pressure
Carbonation
Shot cup is pressurized too. Lid must hold 3 bar, like any soda cap.

Main risk

Alcohol wets many membranes, and a wet membrane can let liquid through. It needs an oil-repellent grade, tested against 40% spirit. It also adds a supplier and cost.

First test

Get vent samples from a membrane supplier, soak them in vodka for two weeks, then check whether they still block liquid at 3 bar.

What I'd build

A1 + A2 + A3 on one pin: a funnel gate with a flap-back and a hiss notch.

A2 is the piece that makes drinking through the shot cup practical, so it belongs in every version. A3 costs nothing in parts and turns the pressure problem into a feature. Click, hiss, pull is the most grenade-like moment you could design into the pin.

Keep A4 as a backup if the hiss notch can't vent reliably or sprays foam. For still flavors, A1 + A2 alone may be enough.

Bench order

  1. A1 still-water rig: confirm a full 44 mL drops through the throat, and time it.
  2. Drinkability: sip through the empty shot cup. Check flow, lip feel, and the dribble from the funnel.
  3. Add the A2 flap and repeat the 20-tilt drip count.
  4. Carbonate to about 3 bar and measure pull force on plain A1.
  5. Add the A3 groove and measure the pull again, then repeat after shaking.
  6. Drop and storage: sealed drops from waist height, and a week at 40 °C, before trusting any seal.
