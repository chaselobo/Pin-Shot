How the pull could work

Three mechanisms that fit the bottle you already have: squat PET body, black flip lid, orange ring on the side of the cap. Each cutaway runs the full sequence. Press Play or drag the slider. Numbers on the drawing match the steps beside it.

A

Gate Pin

The pin itself is the stopper. Your current direction.

One pullPin gets wetLeaves a slot
Order: air channel opens → outlet opens, shot drops → lid snaps open → ring comes free

How it works

  1. Ring, stem and a flat paddle are one molded piece. The whole thing comes free.
  2. The paddle presses up against a gasket ring under the shot cup's outlet. That gasket is the only thing keeping liquor and mixer apart on the shelf.
  3. The same paddle blocks the air-return channel, so nothing mixes if a case ships on its side.
  4. A slit seal in the cap wall wipes the paddle on the way out and is meant to close behind it.
  5. The stem runs through the lid's latch tab. When it clears the tab, the lid is free.
  6. A spring hinge molded into the lid flips it open.

One pull
Storage seal
Sliding gasket, under constant load for months
Hole left
Paddle-sized slot, roughly 3 × 20 mm, into the drink
Carbonation
2–4 bar from the mixer pushes on the paddle from below the whole time
Parts
Ring-paddle, gasket, slit seal, cap, lid

Main risk

The slot. Once the paddle is out, tipping the bottle to drink sends liquid straight at that seal. A gasket slowly flattening over shelf life is the second risk.

First test

Print the cap, fit a silicone slit valve in the wall, fill with dyed water, pull, then hold it at 90° for a minute and look for drips.

B

Peel Strip

The ring pulls a foil strip off the outlet, like opening a yogurt lid.

One pullHeat-sealed storageThin slit left
Order: peel reaches outlet, shot drops → vent hole opens → strip clears latch, lid snaps → ring and strip come free

How it works

  1. The ring is bonded to a foil-laminate strip. Ring and strip come away as one piece.
  2. The strip is heat-sealed across the bottom of the shot cup, covering the outlet and a small vent hole.
  3. It folds back under itself, so pulling peels it at 180°. The peel front moves at half the speed of your hand, which gives a steady, low force.
  4. The peel uncovers the outlet first, then the vent. Air enters through the vent tube and the shot drains cleanly.
  5. The strip leaves through a thin slit with a lip seal. A 0.1 mm strip is much easier to seal around than a rigid paddle.
  6. The strip's tail passes through the lid's latch tab last, so the lid can't open until the peel is finished. Then the spring hinge flips it.
Actions
One pull, slightly longer stroke than A
Storage seal
Heat seal, the same kind used on coffee pods and yogurt cups
Hole left
Thin slit, roughly 0.3 × 15 mm
Carbonation
Heat seal holds pressure well. The lip seal around the strip is the weak point.
Parts
Ring + strip, lip seal, cap, lid

Main risk

The strip tearing instead of peeling. It needs a peelable seal layer and a tough backing film. The slit seal under bottle pressure is the other unknown.

First test

Buy peelable lidding film and an impulse sealer, seal it over a printed cup with a hole, and pull it with a luggage scale to measure force and see whether it tears.

C

Pull & Slam

The pin is a safety, like a real grenade pin. A slap on the lid cuts open a sealed shot pod.

Two actionsPin stays dryNo new hole
Order: pull pin (safety off) → slam: foil cut, shot drops → lid flips open

How it works

  1. The pin runs through the lid's latch tab and a lug on the outside of the cap. It never touches liquid. While it's in, the lid can't move up or down.
  2. The shot sits in a sealed pod with a flexible dome on top. The pod can be filled and sealed on its own, then pressed into the cap.
  3. A hollow cutter hangs from the dome a few millimeters above the floor.
  4. The pod's floor is foil. Slamming the lid inverts the dome, and the cutter slices a C-shaped flap that stays attached. The hollow cutter lets air in.
  5. The same press unhooks the lid from the lug. The spring hinge flips it open.
  6. The hinge is the only joint. The dome and cutter stay in the cap.
Actions
Pull, then slap the top
Storage seal
Fully sealed pod, no moving seals
Hole left
None
Carbonation
Pod is isolated. The cap holds pressure like an ordinary soda closure.
Parts
Pin, pod with dome and cutter, cap, lid

Main risk

It changes your one-pull idea into two moves. The dome has to invert every time from a casual palm slap, and still survive a drop without firing (the pin helps here).

First test

Buy a few push-to-release supplement caps, which use this dome, cutter and foil layout already. Take them apart and scale the pod up to about 50 mL.

What I'd build first

Prototype B and C side by side. Make A as well, because it's cheap and gives a baseline.

B keeps the one-pull ritual and swaps the sliding gasket for a heat seal, which is far more trustworthy over months on a shelf. C is the safe fallback. Nothing crosses the liquid boundary and the parts are already proven in other products. The cost is a second motion. If B's slit seal leaks under pressure, C is where you land.

A is the simplest to understand, but it puts a rigid, wet part through the wall and asks one gasket to do everything.

Bench prototypes, water and food dye

  1. One printed cap body with swappable inserts for A, B and C, on a wide-mouth PET bottle.
  2. Delivered shot: fill 44 mL, pull, and measure what reaches the mixer and how long it takes.
  3. Tip test: after opening, hold at 90° for 60 s and check for leaks anywhere except the spout.
  4. Pull or slap force: luggage scale. Note what feels satisfying and what feels like a struggle.
  5. Pressure: use a carbonation cap and a bike pump to hold about 3 bar overnight, then check every seal.
  6. Drop: sealed, from waist height, five times on each face. Nothing should release.

These are schematic cross-sections. Proportions are close (about 50 mL usable shot cup, 44 mL fill) but they are not CAD. Seal performance, forces and shelf life are unproven until the bench tests above are run.
