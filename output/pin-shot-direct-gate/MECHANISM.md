# PIN SHOT: direct removable gate with a full-shot reservoir

## Current direction

The ring-pin itself forms the barrier between liquor and mixer. Its internal end is a smooth flat paddle sliding beneath the reservoir outlet against a fixed gasket. Pulling removes the entire ring, stem and gate. This replaces the previous ramp/diaphragm/membrane-opener concept.

Preserve the approved affordable-looking PET body, bright sleeve, black retained flip lid and orange ring. The reservoir and ring position must follow actual fluid geometry, even if this requires a deeper neck/collar and a lower ring location.

## Shot capacity

Assumed serving: 1.5 U.S. fluid ounces of liquor, equal to 44.3603 mL; nominal 44.4 mL fill before determining process tolerance and retained-liquid allowance. A 1.5 fl oz serving of 40% ABV spirits is the U.S. standard-drink example:
https://rethinkingdrinking.niaaa.nih.gov/how-much-too-much/whats-standard-drink

Shots vary by market. This project currently uses the U.S. 1.5 fl oz design target; the term shot alone does not specify volume internationally.

Target approximately 50–55 mL usable reservoir space, after subtracting intruding features, to leave headspace above the liquor fill. As a rough size comparison, a plain 40 mm internal-diameter cylinder 40 mm deep encloses 50.27 mL before accounting for its sloped floor, seals and air passage. That comparison is not a final reservoir specification or a promise of 50 mL usable capacity.

The shot reservoir extends into the neck/shoulder. A small cosmetic pod in the original rendering is not evidence of shot capacity. Final mixer fill must also leave space for the released liquor and necessary package headspace.

## Direct gate arrangement

- Mold a bottom drain in a sloped reservoir floor.
- Seal that drain with the inserted flat gate and a fixed compatible gasket.
- Keep the gate, its stem, side exit and ring on one straight withdrawal axis at the reservoir floor height. The illustration remains schematic and its exact geometry is not a CAD definition.
- Provide an air-return path from main-bottle headspace to reservoir headspace. Close its opening with the same gate during storage, so it cannot connect the two liquids when tilted.
- Gate withdrawal uncovers both outlet and air-return path.
- A self-closing slit-seal assembly at the side exit is the proposed way to prevent an additional exterior liquid path after removal. Its actual leakage, wiping, friction, chemical compatibility and pressure behavior are not established.
- Extend the lid's external latch feature to the gate level, so the final withdrawal can release the attached flip lid. The lid spring/flexure and release timing remain to be developed.
- Maintain an unobstructed drinking passage around the reservoir.

Unlike the prior dry-channel cam pin, this gate contacts the liquor. It may emerge wet; do not claim a dry pin. An exit wiper can reduce wetting but must be tested.

## Required behavior

Stored: liquor and mixer remain isolated in any expected shipping orientation.

Pull: the user fully removes one molded ring/gate piece. It opens the reservoir directly. The lid releases as part of the same intended action.

After pull: liquor drains inside the bottle; no loose internal pieces; no persistent exterior opening at the gate exit. The retained lid leaves the drinking opening accessible.

Carbonated variants require a pressure-rated body and closure, controlled venting and lid restraint. Fast pulling cannot be assumed to leave enough time for venting. This is unresolved in the concept.

## Verifying a full shot reaches the mixer

1. Measure the finished reservoir's usable volume.
2. Define factory fill target and tolerances around the desired delivered shot.
3. Measure filled liquor and liquid retained after actuation, using a defined upright drainage time.
4. Check full and partial pulls, temperature, tilt and seal friction.
5. Adjust floor slope, outlet, vent path and fill allowance so the delivered volume meets the eventual specification.
6. Separately measure mixing uniformity; releasing the shot does not demonstrate uniform mixing.

## What has and has not been done

Created a visual design study and functional concept. No physical prototype, liquid-capacity measurement, pressure test, manufacturing CAD, quoted cost or production qualification has been completed. The self-closing exit seal is the largest unresolved issue for a fully removable direct barrier.
